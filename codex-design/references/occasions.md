# Occasions, culture, legal, AI labels, accessibility

Research date 2026-09-23 (`references/research/R7-culture-legal-access.md`). Not legal advice: the rules below are the
safe defaults; the client decides risk.
**The market, language and calendar come from the client and audience, never from the requester's chat language,
location or clock.**

## 1. Special-day posts

### 1.1 Before designing

1. **Occasion record:** the official name per locale, the tone class (below), the date **verified for that market and
   year** with a source link, allowed and banned phrases, approved symbols, commercial allowance (none / soft / full).
2. **Dates move by country:** lunar and moon-sighting days differ (2026: Eid al-Fitr 20 Mar in Saudi Arabia, 21 Mar in
   Bangladesh and Singapore; Eid al-Adha 27 May in Singapore, 28 May in Bangladesh; Vesak 1 May for the UN and
   Bangladesh, 31 May in Singapore). Without a verified date make the creative undated, or two variants (X and X+1),
   and publish after the announcement.
3. **Collisions:** check the same market for clashing days (8 Nov 2026: Diwali and UK Remembrance Sunday; 26 Mar 2027:
   Good Friday and Bangladesh Independence Day) and check the news on the day (national mourning, disasters).
4. **Names by market:** Chinese New Year / 春节 (CN, SG, MY), Seollal (KR), Tết (VN), "Lunar New Year" for mixed Western
   audiences; Diwali / Deepavali; Eid ul-Azha spellings per locale.

### 1.2 Tone classes

| Class | Examples | Greeting | Commerce | Look |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Celebratory | Eid, Diwali, Lunar New Year, Christmas, Pohela Boishakh, independence days | yes ("Eid Mubarak", "Happy Diwali", "শুভ নববর্ষ") | soft; a festive offer only as a **separate** creative | festival palette, correct symbols |
| Commemorative-celebratory | Bangladesh 26 Mar and 16 Dec, US Independence Day, Juneteenth, International Women's Day | greeting + tribute | low-key | dignified festive |
| **Solemn / memorial** | Bangladesh 21 Feb, 14 Dec, 25 Mar; Holocaust Remembrance; Remembrance Day/Sunday; US Memorial Day, 9/11; Ashura; Good Friday; Qingming; Hiroshima/Nagasaki | **never** "Happy", "Congratulations", "Celebrate", "Mubarak", "শুভ"; no exclamation marks, no emoji | **none**: no products, prices, offers, codes, CTAs, contests | muted/monochrome, a customary symbol (flowers, candle, wreath, a traced memorial silhouette); logo small, mono or omitted |
| Fast / penitential | Ramadan daytime, Yom Kippur, Lent | the customary wish ("Ramadan Kareem", "G'mar chatima tova", "Have an easy fast"), never "Happy Yom Kippur" | minimal; no food or drink aimed at fasting hours | calm |
| Contested / political | national days that changed recently, Australia Day, Columbus Day, anything on Kashmir, Taiwan, Israel/Palestine | only on the client's explicit decision | none | neutral |
| Unscheduled mourning | death of a head of state, disasters, attacks | no | pause scheduled promotion | n/a |

`render --occasion solemn` (or `fast`) enforces the words and no-selling rules in the checks.

### 1.3 Structure

Patterns: `day-post.html` (celebratory; a `bilingual` class adds the audience's script) and `day-solemn.html`
(memorial; a laurel wreath drawn from geometry, to be swapped for the day's own customary symbol).


- **Celebratory:** greeting headline in the audience's script, typeset (never generated), optional second language line →
  one correct hero motif from **one** tradition (the festival palette leads, brand colours support) → one warm line
  (8–20 words: gratitude, togetherness, the day's meaning; no feature list) → small logo → caption with the greeting,
  one line, 1–3 CamelCase hashtags, and alt text.
- **Solemn:** the official name of the day → a tribute phrase ("We remember", "In humble tribute", "শ্রদ্ধাঞ্জলি") →
  restrained palette → an accurate symbol → the logo small or omitted; no URL, CTA, price or product.
- Bangladesh examples (native review before use): 21 Feb: "অমর একুশে", "মহান শহীদ দিবস ও আন্তর্জাতিক মাতৃভাষা দিবস",
  "ভাষা শহীদদের প্রতি বিনম্র শ্রদ্ধা"; celebratory: "স্বাধীনতা দিবসের শুভেচ্ছা", "বিজয় দিবসের শুভেচ্ছা", "ঈদ মোবারক",
  "শুভ নববর্ষ ১৪৩৪", "শুভ শারদীয়া", "শুভ বুদ্ধ পূর্ণিমা", "শুভ বড়দিন". A global brand may say "Happy International Mother
  Language Day" to a non-Bengali audience; for a Bangladeshi or Bengali audience 21 Feb is a day of mourning (the flag
  flies at half-mast).

### 1.4 Symbols, flags, maps, sacred text

- **Never generated by the image model:** flags, national emblems, maps, monuments and sacred or religious text. Build
  flags as SVG from the official specification; use licensed photos or traced vector silhouettes of monuments; use the
  client's verified calligraphy for religious phrases.
- **Bangladesh flag:** 10:6; red disc radius = 1/5 of the length; disc centre at 9/20 of the length from the hoist, on
  the horizontal centre line (offset toward the pole). Record one hex pair in the brand kit (commonly #006A4E /
  #F42A41). Red on that green is ~1.66:1: never red text on green; white on green ~6.6:1.
- **India:** 3:2, three equal bands (saffron on top), navy Ashoka Chakra with 24 spokes. **EU:** 12 gold stars in a
  circle. **US:** 50 stars, 13 stripes. **Saudi:** the Shahada; never on merchandise or things that are worn, kicked or
  discarded; never at half-mast. **Japan:** a centred disc; the rays are a different flag, never for KR/CN audiences.
- **Flag misuse:** no text or logos on a flag; not as clothing or on disposable items; US Flag Code: "never used for
  advertising purposes"; India: no commercial misuse (penalised).
- **Maps:** none in marketing art unless the client supplies an approved market map (disputed borders cause backlash).
- **One tradition per creative:** a hanukkiah has 9 branches (not the 7-branch menorah, no Christmas mix); Eid art
  never uses the Taj Mahal (a mausoleum), church spires, alcohol, daytime eating in Ramadan, or Qur'anic verses as
  decoration; Diwali uses diyas, rangoli, marigolds (Chinese lanterns are not its hero motif; deities never on floors,
  feet or footwear); Vesak places the Buddha high and never near alcohol; Lunar New Year uses the correct year animal
  (2027: Goat/Sheep; Vietnam uses the Cat for the Rabbit year) and avoids the number 4; Día de Muertos is not Halloween.
- **Blocked by market:** swastika forms for Western audiences, Rising Sun rays for KR/CN, 88/1488 as promo numbers,
  the number 4 in East Asian prices (warn). Check every generated hand for gestures that offend in the market
  (OK sign, thumbs-up, V-sign, left hand for eating).

## 2. Legal and platform policy (safe defaults)

- **Third-party marks:** no other brands' logos or trademarks without a permission record (official badge rules only);
  no event marks for non-sponsors (Olympic rings and words, World Cup, Super Bowl, mascots, trophies).
- **People:** no real or look-alike people without a release; no celebrity names in prompts; generated people are never
  presented as real staff, customers or patients.
- **Copyright:** no copyrighted characters; no "in the style of <living artist/studio/brand>" prompts. A purely
  AI-generated logo is not copyrightable in the US: the final mark is human-drawn (`brand.md`).
- **Fonts:** every font has a licence entry (the `fonts` command writes FONT-LICENSES.md); OFL fonts may be embedded;
  never embed fonts whose embedding flag forbids it; never use legacy non-Unicode fonts (Bijoy/SutonnyMJ).
- **Testimonials and reviews:** only real quotes with a permission record; never AI faces, names or quotes in a
  testimonial layout (the FTC bans AI fake reviews); star ratings need a source and date.
- **Claims:** no invented prices, discounts, awards, certifications, statistics or "No. 1/best" claims; "was/now"
  prices only with a real reference price and dates; health, finance and beauty: no cure words, "guaranteed",
  "risk-free", "instant"; no generated before/after images (TikTok bans them in ads; Meta restricts them).
- **Contests:** show or link the T&Cs, the promoter and the closing date; Facebook entries cannot require sharing or
  tagging.
- **Ads:** no fake UI (play, download, close buttons, fake notifications or input fields); no sensational fear
  appeals or excessive caps; Google responsive/PMax image assets are clean images (no overlaid text, logo or buttons);
  marketplace main images (Google Merchant Center, eBay, Amazon) carry no promotional overlays, text, badges or
  watermarks. Political or social-issue ads: stop and escalate.
- **Hiring posts:** include the pay range where pay-transparency rules apply (EU).

## 3. AI disclosure (2026)

- **EU AI Act Article 50** applies since 2 Aug 2026 to content meant for EU audiences, including the open internet;
  the agency is usually the "deployer". Realistic synthetic people count as deep fakes even when fictional; so does an
  AI product render that could mislead about the real product. An AI background behind a real product is fine if it
  does not mislead; fantastical scenes are out of scope. **A visible label is required** for deep fakes: metadata alone
  never satisfies it.
- **Tiers for our work:**
  1. AI-assisted standard edits (background removal or extension for looks, colour, cleanup): no label.
  2. Stylised or illustrative AI visuals composited with type: no EU label; TikTok ads still need their AI disclosure;
     Meta may show "AI info".
  3. Photorealistic AI people, places, products or events: a **burned-in label** ("AI-generated image", top corner,
     legible at display size, text ≥ 4.5:1) for EU audiences, plus alt-text mention and the platform toggle; New York
     ads with an AI person need a conspicuous disclosure; China and India need labels too.
- **Never:** photorealistic AI depictions of real tragedies, wars, disasters or news events; an AI render of a product
  that differs from the real one; stripping C2PA/SynthID; fake camera EXIF.
- **Metadata:** raw AI images keep `trainedAlgorithmicMedia` (codex-imagegen); finished designs with AI elements carry
  `compositeSynthetic` (the renderer writes it); AI-edited client photos are `compositeWithTrainedAlgorithmicMedia`.
  Social platforms strip metadata, which is why the visible label matters when it is required.
- **Tell the client in writing:** what is AI, what is labelled and why, that platforms (Meta, TikTok, LinkedIn,
  Pinterest) may auto-label it, and that labels must not be removed downstream.
- **AI-drafted copy:** ordinary ad copy needs no AI-text label; health, safety, sustainability or investor claims need
  expert review and a named editor.

## 4. Accessibility

- Contrast ≥ 4.5:1 (small) / 3:1 (large and meaningful graphics) on the worst pixels (the renderer measures it).
- Text at display size: on a 1080-wide canvas body ≥ 40 px, headlines ≥ 72 px, fine print ≥ 30 px and repeated in the
  caption; +10–20 % for complex scripts. The presets carry these floors.
- **Alt text for every exported image**, in the post's language: what it shows and why it matters, including every
  piece of essential text in the image (≤ ~125 characters for the core, more for text-heavy graphics); never "image
  of". Put it in the delivery notes next to each file.
- Essential information (date, time, place, price, URL, T&Cs) is also in the caption or post text.
- Never encode meaning in colour alone or red vs green; check `render --simulate deuteranopia,achromatopsia`.
- RTL: `dir` + `lang`, mirrored layout, no letter-spacing on Arabic, logos and photos not mirrored, digits per market.
- Non-Latin text is typeset with a proper Unicode font and the right `lang`, and read by a native reader.
- Print: body ≥ 12 pt for general audiences (≥ 16 pt large print), lines 50–65 characters; tagged PDFs with a language
  and reading order for brochures delivered digitally.

## 5. Colour, number and gesture meanings (check per market)

- **White:** mourning in parts of East and South Asia; bridal in the West. **Red:** luck and celebration in China,
  danger or sale in the West, also mourning in parts of Africa. **Green:** Islam, prosperity; avoid a green hat in
  China. **Black:** mourning in the West and many markets. **Yellow/saffron:** sacred in Hinduism and Buddhism;
  imperial in China. **Purple:** mourning in Thailand and parts of Latin America.
- **Numbers:** 4 is unlucky in China, Japan and Korea; 8 lucky in China; 13 unlucky in the West; 17 in Italy.
- **Gestures:** the OK sign, thumbs-up and V-sign (palm in) are insults in some markets; left-hand eating or giving is
  impolite in South Asia and the Middle East.
- **Imagery:** feet and soles toward people or sacred things; dogs and pigs in Muslim-majority markets; alcohol near
  religious themes; modest dress norms by market.
