# R10 — What looks current and what audiences prefer: design trends, platform data, anti-AI attitudes, type, layout and industry playbooks (late 2026)

Research date: 2026-09-23. Every source was accessed on that day. Scope: the taste layer of the codex-design skill. It covers what makes social posts, stories, carousels, YouTube thumbnails, banners, covers, ads, flyers, posters and infographics look current, like top-1% human work rather than an AI template, and what audiences demonstrably prefer in 2025–2026.

## Summary: 15 findings

1. **Human proof now beats polish, and the platforms say so.**
   - Instagram's head argued on 2025-12-31 that AI has made polish cheap. He said raw, even unflattering imagery is becoming the proof that a human made something, and that it will be easier to fingerprint real media than to detect fake media [24].
   - Getty's mid-2026 check-in reports that flawlessness now reads as *less* credible, while imperfection, craft and lived experience signal trust [37].
   - Landor calls the 2026 design response "Anti-AI Crafting" [25].
   - For the skill: every piece needs at least one visible real or human element.

2. **Audiences penalise content they *perceive* as AI, even when it is not.**
   - Raptive: suspected-AI content lost about half of reader trust, including when it was human-written, and adjacent ad performance fell 14 % [40].
   - A USC/Journal of Consumer Research study of 1M+ TikTok posts and 8 experiments: AI disclosures cut engagement because viewers infer less creator effort [43].
   - NIQ: AI ads produced weaker memory activation [39].
   - TBWA\ and Ideally: disclosure deepens the penalty, and high-trust sectors suffer most [41, UNVERIFIED details].
   - So the job is to not *look* machine-average. The job is not to hide AI (R7 governs disclosure).

3. **"Slop" is mainstream, and platforms now let users filter it out.**
   - Merriam-Webster's 2025 word of the year was "slop" [48].
   - Since 2025-10-16, Pinterest lets users see fewer generative-AI pins in beauty, art, fashion and home décor [20].
   - Brands now advertise "no AI": Dove, Aerie, Cadbury, Polaroid, Equinox, Almond Breeze, LEGO and Coterie [45][46][47].
   - AI-looking visuals therefore carry both a distribution risk and a brand-perception risk.

4. **Carousels are the top organic format on Instagram and LinkedIn, but not on TikTok.**
   - Instagram, Q2 2026: carousels 0.50 %, Reels 0.48 %, images 0.33 % [3].
   - Carousels earn 9× the saves of single images [7].
   - LinkedIn PDF carousels: median 21.77 % engagement vs 6.52 % for images [1].
   - TikTok photo posts: 1.92 % vs 3.39 % for video [1]. Video gets 5× the reach [9].

5. **Single static images are the weakest organic format and still declining.**
   - Instagram single-image reach fell 22 % and engagement 46 % year on year [7]. Socialinsider shows images falling 17 % [3].
   - Exceptions:
     - Facebook, where all formats sit within about one point of each other [1].
     - Paid Meta, where simple statics are among the common winners [22].
   - A single image must carry one share-worthy idea.

6. **Faces help YouTube thumbnails less than the folklore claims.**
   - vidIQ, 500 breakouts, June 2026 [13]:
     - 69 % used a face (80 % of the top 50);
     - only 5 % used an exaggerated expression;
     - 28 % used no text at all, and the median was 5 words when text was used;
     - 56 % were high contrast.
   - 1of10, 300k viral videos from 2025: faces perform about the same as no faces overall. Multiple faces beat a single face. Faces helped in finance and hurt in business [14].

7. **Pinterest rewards native-looking pins over branded ones.**
   - Viral pins split roughly 50/50 between raw and designed images. Only 4 % of viral designed pins used the brand's palette [16].
   - Pinterest's own advice: put the visual in the middle, key text at the top and details at the bottom, with the product in a realistic setting [17].

8. **Type is the hero again, and the decade of neutral sans is ending.**
   - Rising styles:
     - high-contrast serifs;
     - "mutant heritage" grotesks;
     - '60s–'70s ITC-style revivals;
     - curvy serifs;
     - liquid italics;
     - handwritten micro-doses [26][35][34].
   - Apple Music's new bold, retro, lowercase wordmark (2026-09-22) was read by the design press as the end of minimalism [29].

9. **Designers' own "so over" list** (Creative Boom, 2026-04-21) [30]:
   - AI caricature trends (Ghibli, action figure);
   - glassmorphism and Liquid Glass copies;
   - decorative gradients and four-point "AI sparkle" stars;
   - the AI yellow cast;
   - empty minimalism and unreadable maximalism;
   - beige Canva templates;
   - Y2K chrome;
   - bento grids;
   - motion for its own sake.

   Add from other sources:
   - '90s-style cartoon food mascots (Dieline [34]);
   - the spreading grey-and-white lowercase "rhode" look in beauty [58].

10. **The rising layouts share one root: specificity plus visible making.**
    - Editorial or magazine layouts.
    - Scrapbook and scanner collage with handwritten notes.
    - Direct-flash photography and "ambient realism".
    - Hyper-chromatic colour.
    - "Minimaximalism" (a quiet structure with one loud element).
    - Mascot worlds.
    - Soft edges.
    - Sources: [31][32][34][51].

11. **Paid social: simple, varied statics win, and context beats format.**
    - Motion analysed $1.29B of Meta spend and 578,750 creatives. About 5 % of creatives become "winners", and those winners take 55 % of spend [21].
    - Text-only ads, product-plus-text-overlay images and simple GIFs are common top performers [22].
    - The best format differs across 16 verticals [21].
    - For the skill: deliver several genuinely different concepts, not variations of one.

12. **LinkedIn is becoming personal and visual.**
    - Personal profiles earn 63 % more engagement than company pages [8].
    - LinkedIn says one relevant image can lift reactions by up to 40 % [23].
    - About 4 in 10 consumers (62 % of Gen Z) discover products through employee-generated content [50].
    - For the skill: design people-led templates, not logo-first posts.

13. **Colour: an off-white base with saturated accents.**
    - The base: Pantone Cloud Dancer (R4).
    - The accents:
      - Stocksy's "Hyper Chromatic" [31];
      - Envato's "saturation revival" [36];
      - WGSN's Transformative Teal [62];
      - sport and fan colour codes in a World Cup year [19][37].
    - Peer-reviewed work links colour complexity to higher engagement [56].
    - Avoid the AI purple-blue gradient and the warm AI cast [30][57].

14. **Industries split along trust lines.**
    - **Trust sectors** (dental, clinics, finance, B2B) are hit hardest by AI penalties [41]. They should lead with real people and places, in a "clinical with soul" calm [34].
    - **Lifestyle sectors** (food, fitness, retail, beauty) can push joy, maximalism, mascots and handmade type [25][26][34].
    - **Beauty's** minimal template is saturated, so it now needs character [58].

15. **Freshness is about intention, not effects.** Every 2026 critique converges on the same complaint: work made "without thought" [30][27]. §7 turns this into 20 current signals and 20 dated signals that a judge can check.

### 1.4 Colour for late 2026 (brief)

- **Bases.**
  - Paper and off-white (Cloud Dancer family) for calm, premium, health and editorial work.
  - Deep inks: navy, oxblood, forest.
  - Transformative Teal as a mid-tone [62].
- **Accents.** Saturated and slightly unexpected pairings: hyper-chromatic [31], saturation revival [36], "intense joy" clashes [26].
- **Seasonal codes.**
  - Team and jersey colours, sport-luxe [19].
  - Sailor blue and white stripes [19].
  - Frosted and smoky beauty tones [19].
- **Evidence for complexity.** In a peer-reviewed study, images with more colour variation drew more engagement. The effect depended on time of day, image height and caption sentiment [56]. **[OPINION]** Colour complexity belongs in the photo or illustration. Keep the type layer simple, with 1–2 colours.
- **Avoid.**
  - The AI purple→blue gradient.
  - AI cyan on black.
  - The warm AI cast.
  - Beige on beige.
  - Using a trend colour as the brand base (R4).

**Design implications**
- **[OPINION] Default to a 1080×1350 or 1080×1440 carousel for any teaching, list, story or range content.**
  - Slide 1 is the hook and must survive the 3:4 grid crop (R5).
  - Each slide carries one idea and must stand alone, because carousels resurface on later slides [3].
- **Design for saves and sends:**
  - frameworks and checklists;
  - a "screenshot this" summary slide;
  - numbers;
  - a joke that begs to be shared.

  Reels win comments. Carousels win saves and views [3].
- **Single images.** Keep them for one-shot ideas: announcements, statements, event posters, memes. Make the idea legible at phone size in under 2 s.
- **Faces.** Don't assume a face helps. Test by vertical [55][14].

### 3.5 Implications for the skill

1. Treat "looks AI" as a **conversion risk**, not only a taste issue [39][40][41][43].
2. **Trust sectors** (health, dental, finance, B2B, real estate): no photoreal AI people, and no AI "results" imagery. Use real photography or illustration [41].
3. When AI imagery *is* used, make it clearly illustrative or textural. Composite it with real type and real photos. Follow R7 for labels.
4. Give each piece a **proof slot** (real photo, number with source, named person, or handmade mark). The judge should check for it (§7).

## 4. Typography in current social and banner design

### 4.1 Direction of travel (2025-12 → 2026-09)

- **Character over the neutral sans.** Dalziel & Pow describe film and series titles leading the way (*Wuthering Heights*, *Ballad of a Small Player*) [26].
- **High-contrast serifs are back** as a counter to AI's uniform polish:
  - LittleBird uses Meraki;
  - Flamingo Estate uses Exposure VAR;
  - Post Post Hotel uses BR-Post;
  - Olive + Gourmando uses HAL Timezone [26].
- **"Mutant heritage."** Old serifs and mid-century grotesks, slightly off-kilter: Klim's Die Grotesk, ALT's Erogenous, Stockholm Design Lab's Sigma [26].
- **'60s–'70s ITC and photo-lettering revivals.** Big x-heights, tight letterfit (Apex Wheels with ITC Avant Garde, Thorlo, Residence) [26].
- **Curvy, "funky" serifs** (Affinity rebrand, Eventbrite's bouncy serif) and **liquid or bubbled italics** (Tilt Beauty, SKIMS) [35].
- **Other type moods** [35]:
  - quiet, elegant sans for luxury;
  - "Italiano" retro type for food;
  - sharp "cutting edge" letterforms;
  - type as graphic (patterns, frames);
  - cute and cosy rounded faces;
  - chaotic scripts.
- **Friction and noise** as a sign of an authentic point of view (Frere-Jones Type). **"Intense joy"**: clashing, bright type for restaurants and venues [26].
- **Handwritten micro-dose** inside polished layouts: jittery baselines, ink bleed [34].
- **Kinetic type** (Spotify Wrapped) [26]. The backlash against motion for motion's sake runs in parallel [30].
- **Bold lowercase retro wordmarks.** Apple Music (2026-09-22) replaced SF with a bold lowercase retro wordmark [29].

### 4.2 Technique table

All rules are [OPINION], built on R4's size maths (1080 canvas shown at 0.33–0.41×), unless cited.

| Technique | Status, late 2026 | Works best for | Rules that keep it legible on a phone | Failure modes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Oversized type as the image** (1–4 words filling 40–70 % of the canvas) | Rising [25][51] | Launches, statements, event posters, covers, thumbnails | Cap height ≥160 px on 1080. Tighten tracking −1 to −3 %. Line height 0.85–1.0. One colour on a solid or photo field | Breaking a word mid-glyph at the canvas edge on a feed crop |
| **Text behind the subject** (depth layering) | Mainstream on Instagram since 2024–25; still popular [52] | Portraits, athletes, products with a tall silhouette, event hero shots | 1–2 words only, ≥180 px caps. Hide ≤25–30 % of the word's area, never the first or last letter, keep the x-height band readable. The cut-out needs clean edges (hair!) | Unreadable word; a cheap-looking one-click cut-out; overused on templates |
| **Knockout / masked type** (image inside the letters) | Steady | Travel, food, event names | Weight ≥700, caps ≥140 px. Only low-detail image areas inside the letters. Add a thin outline if the edges vanish | Busy textures destroy the letter shapes at small scale |
| **Overlapping type and image layers** (a cut-out overlapping the headline) | Rising (styles.md "Bold Committed") | Product launches, sport, fashion | Overlap ≤20 % of the headline. Keep a consistent light direction and a soft contact shadow | Depth that contradicts itself |
| **Condensed display caps** | Stable | Sport, events, news, sale posts | Great for long words in narrow columns. Open tracking +2–5 % when caps are small | Too thin at small sizes (use weight ≥600) |
| **High-contrast serif display** | Rising [26] | Premium, real estate, beauty, editorial carousels, finance | Keep hairlines ≥3 px on 1080. | **'70s ITC / curvy serif revival** | Rising [26][35] | Food, drinks, youth retail, music, events | Pair with a plain sans for details. Reinterpret with modern spacing, not as pastiche | Reads as a throwback when paired with sepia and a retro grade |
| **Liquid or bubbled italics** | Rising in youth beauty and fashion [35] | Beauty, fashion, Gen Z DTC | Display only; body stays upright. Keep plenty of white space | Legibility collapses in light weights |
| **Mixed weights and scale jumps in one line** | Stable | Headlines with a key word | Keep ≥2 weight steps between them (e.g. 300 vs 800) so the contrast survives 0.33× scale | Random emphasis |
| **Outlined (stroke-only) text** | Declining as a primary style | An echo line behind a solid line; sport | Stroke ≥5–6 px at 1080. Never for body text or the key word | Disappears at phone size; reads as a template |
| **Caption boxes / text boxes** (the TikTok-native "classic" look, highlighter bars) | Current in lo-fi and native contexts [23][54] | Reels and TikTok covers, lo-fi ads, stories | Solid box (not glass), padding 0.35–0.5 em, one line per box, TikTok Sans or brand sans [53] | Glassy or gradient boxes |
| **UI-native type** (Notes app, iMessage, comment-reply sticker, search bar) | Current, especially in ads (R4: Canva "Notes App Chic" +90 %) | Paid social, creator-style posts, education | Use a real system-font look and plausible UI. Never fake interactive buttons (a TikTok ad-policy issue, R7) | Fake UI is a policy risk; overused in DTC |
| **Handwritten annotations** (arrows, circles, marginal notes) | Rising [34][32] | Carousels, education, food, behind the scenes | ≤5 words, ≥44 px. Real strokes, used once per slide | Script fonts with repeated glyphs |
| **Static stand-ins for kinetic type**: directional motion blur on one word, stretch or squash via a width axis, step-and-repeat echoes, split or misregistered letters, type on a path, a film strip of word states | Current for sport and music | Sport, events, launches, music | Keep the motion effect to **one** word; the rest stays static and crisp | Motion as decoration [30] |
| **Lowercase wordmark and voice** | Current in beauty and tech [58][29] | Beauty, lifestyle, apps | Needs a strong typeface; lowercase alone isn't a brand | Rhode clones [58] |

### 4.3 Overused or dated type moves (avoid unless the brief demands them)

- Gradient-filled text, neon glow text and chrome text [30]; R2 and R4 tells.
- Default Inter, Poppins or Montserrat at 400/700 only, and beige Canva "script + sans" pairs [30]; R4.
- A tracked-caps eyebrow over every heading; 01/02/03 labels on things that aren't sequences (R2).
- 2024 "brat" styling: lime with blurred Arial Narrow [OPINION].
- Outline-only headlines; heavy drop-shadowed white type on busy photos; ALL CAPS paragraphs.
- Too many styles per piece: more than 2 families, more than 3 sizes, or more than 1 effect per element (R4).

### 4.4 Readability at phone size: only the rules R4 does not already cover

- **Word budgets.**
  - A thumbnail works with 0–3 words [13].
  - A carousel hook ≤ 8 words; body ≤ 30 words per slide (R5).
  - Pinterest: message at the top, details at the bottom [17].
- **In-app search reads on-image text.** Google indexes public Instagram posts (since July 2025) [23]. **[OPINION]** Put the topic keyword in the headline itself rather than in a decorative word.
- **Judge any effect at display scale.** Text behind the subject, knockouts, outlines and italics must pass a 360-px-wide preview (R4 §1.2), not only the 1080 canvas.
- **Captions.** Burned-in captions and on-screen text are expected (sound-off viewing; LinkedIn's own video advice [23]). Design caption boxes that clear every safe zone (R5).

## 7. Freshness checklist (late 2026)

For the art-director judge. Each signal has a check a vision model can run.

### 7.1 Twenty signals that a design looks current

1. **One idea readable in about 2 s at phone size.** Hook ≤8 words (thumbnail ≤3). *Check:* the 360-px preview is read correctly.
2. **Type is the hero.** A characterful display face (revival serif, '70s geometric, liquid italic, condensed) with a quiet text face. *Check:* the display face isn't a default sans.
3. **Decisive scale contrast.** Headline ≥3× body, or one giant word. *Check:* the ratio of measured cap heights.
4. **Real photography of the client's actual people, place or product.** *Check:* the brief supplied the photo, or the image is non-photoreal illustration.
5. **Camera honesty.** Neutral white balance, real skin texture, plausible light (flash or daylight). *Check:* no yellow or teal-orange cast; skin has pores.
6. **One human mark used with purpose.** A handwritten note, a hand-drawn underline or arrow, a scan, tape or stitch. *Check:* present exactly once or twice, and tied to the content.
7. **Specificity.** Real names, places, prices, dates, and numbers with sources. *Check:* at least one concrete fact that no competitor could share.
8. **A modern base colour.** Paper or off-white, deep ink, or a bold saturated field; neutrals are tinted. *Check:* no pure #000 or #FFF fields and no grey-gradient backgrounds.
9. **One unexpected, confident accent pairing** (hyper-chromatic), or a strict two-ink print look. *Check:* ≤2 accents.
10. **Editorial devices with a function:** captions, folios, a numbered index, pull quotes. *Check:* each device labels something real.
11. **Carousel as a sequence.** A cover hook, one idea per slide, a save-worthy payload slide, one CTA, and slides that stand alone. *Check:* the slide-level rubric.
12. **Native idioms used knowingly** where the channel is lo-fi: caption boxes, Notes app, comment reply. *Check:* the UI is plausible and there are no fake buttons.
13. **A voice with humour or point of view.** *Check:* the copy says something only this brand would say.
14. **An ownable series device** (a frame, colour field, mascot or type treatment) **plus real variation** per post. *Check:* no two posts in a set share both layout and imagery.
15. **Tactile materials** (paper, fabric, clay, ink, print texture) rather than glossy CGI. *Check:* no chrome, glass or gummy renders unless the brief asks.
16. **Evidence layout.** One big number, a chart as proof, a source line. *Check:* the number has a source.
17. **People-led formats.** A real founder or employee face and name where relevant. *Check:* real headshot supplied.
18. **Retro reinterpreted, not costumed.** A revival face with modern spacing and a neutral grade. *Check:* no sepia grade on a retro layout.
19. **Accessibility baked in.** Contrast holds at display scale; captions and alt text supplied. *Check:* the R4 contrast gates.
20. **Restraint in effects.** ≤1 effect per element. Shadows match the scene light. No glow. *Check:* count the effects.

### 7.2 Twenty signals that a design looks dated or like an AI template

1. Purple→blue or neon gradients, gradient-filled text, glow text [30]; R4.
2. Four-point sparkle "AI" stars, glowing orbs, circuit brains, robots, holographic globes [30][37].
3. Glassmorphism panels or Liquid Glass copies over busy photos [30].
4. A warm yellow or sepia cast, a teal-orange HDR grade, waxy or plastic skin [30][57]; R4 §5.2.
5. AI caricature styles (Ghibli-fied, action-figure box, "Pixar you", claymation by prompt) [30].
6. Garbled pseudo-text, invented labels, or fake logos inside imagery (R4).
7. Everything centre-stacked on one axis, often with three rounded cards and thin-line icons (R4, R2).
8. Default fonts (Inter, Poppins, Montserrat) at 400/700 only, or beige Canva script + sans pairs [30].
9. Bento grids and identical stat tiles with no content reason [30].
10. Corporate Memphis people, clay-emoji 3D icons, or mixed icon styles [UNVERIFIED]; R4.
11. Y2K chrome or pixel type as nostalgia wallpaper; 2024 "brat" lime with blurred Arial Narrow [30] [OPINION].
12. Gummy or jelly 3D, glossy 2025 "AI identity" 3D forms [25]; R4.
13. '90s cartoon "food buddy" mascot clones [34].
14. The post is just a mockup: stationery, phone or floating product on a pedestal with fake reflections [30]; R1.
15. Brush-stroke banners, starbursts, ribbon badges, emoji clutter (R4).
16. Weightless copy ("Elevate", "Unlock", "Seamless") and fake-precise stats (R1, R4).
17. A series where only the words change: template sameness. It fails YouTube's inauthentic policy (R2) and audiences' originality bar (43 % say brands aren't original [50]).
18. Stock clichés: handshakes, laughing at salad, pointing at laptops, perfect smiles, lightbulb brains (R4).
19. Fake interface bait: play buttons, "Buy" buttons, notification badges on statics. A policy risk too (R7).
20. Idea-free beige "quiet luxury" minimalism, noisy maximalism with no hierarchy, or a rhode grey/white lowercase clone for a brand that isn't rhode [30][58].

### 7.3 How the judge should score it [OPINION]

- **FAIL** when any of dated signals 1, 4, 5 or 6 is present, or when **≥3** dated signals are present.
- **PASS** needs ≥6 current signals, including #1 and at least one of #4, #6 or #7 (the proof slot).
- Report the weakest current signal as the "one fix" (R4 rubric).
- Refresh this list every quarter. Trend signals decay: bento and glass moved from "current" to "tired" in about 18 months [30].

## 8. What the skill should change (proposals)

1. **Add a freshness gate** (§7) to the art-director judge, next to R4's rubric and R7's lint rules.
2. **Add a required brief field, "proof".** Real photos, a named person, a sourced fact, or a handmade mark. If it is missing, ask for it or choose a non-photoreal illustration route.
3. **Format defaults by platform:**

   | Platform | Default deliverable |
   |---|---|
   | Instagram | Carousel for teaching, lists and ranges; single image only for one-shot ideas |
   | LinkedIn | PDF document or multi-image post |
   | TikTok | Covers, not carousels |
   | Pinterest | 2:3 pin with top/bottom text, photo-derived colour and alt text |
   | YouTube | 3 thumbnail hypotheses: face-led, object/result-led, text-led |

4. **Paid Meta:** always ≥5 distinct concepts, including a text-only headline static and a product + overlay static. Tell the client to switch off Advantage+ overlay enhancements.
5. **Trust sectors** (dental, clinic, finance, B2B, real estate): block photoreal AI people and AI "results". Prefer the "Clinical with Soul" or "Photo-led Minimal" directions.
6. **New direction candidates for `styles.md`** (verify fonts on Google Fonts first):
   - **Scrapbook Scanner:** real scans, tape, handwritten notes, a visual index.
   - **Direct-Flash Editorial:** night photos, hard shadows, magazine type.
   - **Clinical with Soul:** a paper base, claim-first layout, one warm accent, a friendly serif.
   - **Hyper-Chromatic Pop:** a saturated clashing field, giant type, one mascot.
   - **Revival Serif:** high-contrast or '70s ITC-style display, modern spacing.
7. **Add a "fading" file** that the prompt builder reads, so generators are steered away from §7.2 looks. Include negative prompts for the yellow cast, glass, sparkle stars and gummy 3D.
8. **Keep one trend move per piece.** Trend colours stay accents only (R4). Rotate series devices so no two consecutive posts share both layout and image type.
