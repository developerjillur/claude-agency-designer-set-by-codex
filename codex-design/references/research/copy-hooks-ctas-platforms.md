## A. Hook library

- Topic in the first line or first ~2 s (on-screen text is read in ~1 s); no greeting or setup [66][67].
- A number, name or date beats adjectives [64][66], but give just enough: too-vague and too-literal headlines both lose clicks [54].
- Loss words pull: +2.3% CTR per negative word in news headlines, -1.0% per positive [51].
- Hooks and titles as statements: question titles cut engagement [52], except a specific question to "you" [53]. Ask questions at the caption's end [41].
- Video hook = visual + spoken line + on-screen text, never repeating each other [65]. YouTube treats title, thumbnail and first 30 s as one package [32].

- Thumbnail: 2-4 words adding what the title lacks, no shared words; breakout median was 5, legibility drops past 5-6 [31][66].

## C. CTA

1. Verb + what they get + qualifier; drop Submit, Learn more, Click here [65].
2. First person often wins ("Start my free trial" +90%, 2013) - test it [55]. Low effort wins: "Get started" beat "Book a demo" by 111% [57].
3. One ask per asset [66][60].
4. Placement: last carousel slide, Story frame 4 [66]; in video, voiceover plus on-screen text [43]; TikTok CTA cards +45% recall [35].
5. Ask for saves and comments, not likes (Instagram): save CTAs +92% saves, comment CTAs +203% comments; like requests cut likes 4.9% [41].
6. No bait: Meta demotes asks for likes, shares, comments, tags, votes; asking for help, advice or recommendations is exempt [15][16].
7. Urgency only when true; the FTC's dark-patterns report targets fake urgency [58].
8. Comment-keyword to DM: one typeable word, shown on screen, via Instagram's tools or approved partners [66].
9. No CTA on pure reach or mood pieces, replies, sensitive posts (inference).

Library (my wording; rules sourced):
- Buy: "Claim my 20% off" / "Get yours before Friday's restock" (if true) / "Shop the drop: link in bio"
- Register: "Save my seat, Thursday 7 pm" / "Start my 14-day trial" / "Join the free workshop"
- Visit: "See the full menu: link in bio" / "Tap the link sticker for directions"
- Save: "Save this for your next launch" / "Save the checklist before your next shoot"
- Share: "Send this to whoever runs your ads" / "Share with your team before Monday" (safer than "share if you agree"; inference)
- Comment: "Which would you fix first: 1, 2 or 3?" / "Worst brief you've ever received?"
- DM: "Comment GUIDE and I'll DM the template" / "DM AUDIT for a 5-minute review"
- Watch: "Part 2 is pinned" / "Full breakdown on YouTube: link in bio"

## D. Captions

- Hook inside the fold; 2-6 short paragraphs split by blank lines; one ask; tags or none [66].
- Simple words: 5th-7th-grade copy converts at 11.1% vs 5.3% for professional-level [56].
- Search phrases in plain sentences; Instagram search reads captions [66].
- Emoji as punctuation, never first, at most ~4 per 100 characters [66].
- End with one specific question (+37% comments) [41]. Story polls are the cheapest tap; question boxes harvest audience wording [66]. LinkedIn polls underperform (4.20% vs 5.20%) [21].
- Alt text: describe the scene, repeat on-image text verbatim (inference).

## E. Design for attention

1. Glance first: feed content registers in 0.25 s [44], mobile attention averages ~1.7 s [45], memory needs ~2.5 s, which ~85% of ads never get [46]. One focal point, readable instantly.
2. Hierarchy: at most 3 type sizes, 2 large elements, 3 contrast levels [48]. Squint test: only headline, subject and CTA should survive the blur (inference).
3. Contrast and colour: text 4.5:1 or better (3:1 at 24 px+) [49]; one accent colour [66]. 56% of breakout thumbnails used high-contrast colour; avoid YouTube's red, white, black [31].
4. Faces: in 69% of breakout thumbnails at 1/4-1/3 of the frame [31], but neutral across 300k videos, niche-dependent; several beat one [32]. Gaze toward the product draws looks and purchase intent; animated faces steal attention [47].
5. On-image text: 6 words or fewer on covers and thumbnails, ~28-32 px minimum at 1080 wide, ~120 px from every edge [65][66]. Detail belongs in the caption, not the image (inference).
6. Motion: frame 1 moves [66]; dynamic edits and overlays [36]; tight framing, fast pacing [43].
7. Native beats polished: Mosseri calls the polished feed "dead" and favours raw images [8]; creator-made native Reels ads: 5% lower cost per result, 11% higher conversion [13].

## F. Frameworks

- AIDA: full ad or carousel arc [62].
- PAS: short ads and captions for pain-aware buyers [62].
- BAB: transformation posts, before/after graphics [62].
- 4U (Useful, Urgent, Unique, Ultra-specific): score every headline and cover [59].
- FAB: product cards and bullets [62].
- "So what?" x3: the third answer is the benefit [63].
- Rule of One: one idea, emotion, story, benefit, response per asset [60].
- Specificity over superlatives: named details beat platitudes [64].
- Voice of customer: mine reviews for sticky phrases; one mined headline drew 400% more clicks [61].
- ABCD (video): Attract, Brand, Connect, Direct; +30% short-term sales likelihood [43].
- Four hook killers (delay, confusion, irrelevance, disinterest): audit openers [67].
