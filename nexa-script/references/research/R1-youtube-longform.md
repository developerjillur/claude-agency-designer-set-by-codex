# R1: How the best creators plan and script long-form YouTube (2024 to 2026)

Knowledge-base file for the script-writing skill. Compiled 2026-09-26. Covers explainers, documentaries and video essays, story-driven videos, tutorials, commentary and list videos.

**How to read it.** Sources are numbered [S1] to [S30] in the list at the end (publisher, title, URL, date). Evidence tags: **P** = primary (the creator's own words, an official YouTube page, the original paper, or the video's own caption track, which I read); **L** = leaked internal document; **S** = secondary report; **M** = our own measurement (caption tracks, 2026-09-26); **U** = unverified. Everything is paraphrased; quotes are under 15 words, at most one per source.

**Limits.** The search budget ran out partway, so these stay U: a Mark Rober "CURIOSITY" acronym, "the valley" as a named retention concept, Ali Abdaal's own scripting articles (his site blocked automated reading) and the Pew 2019 video-length study (blocked).

## 0. Ten findings in one screen

1. YouTube's own title and thumbnail test picks the winner by watch time share, not clicks [S4].
2. YouTube's "Intro" metric is the share still watching at 0:30; its fix for a weak intro is to align title and thumbnail with the video and rework the first 30 seconds [S2].
3. Recommendations also use satisfaction surveys, likes, dislikes and shares, so a regretted click is a negative signal [S5][S6].
4. The leaked MrBeast doc plans retention by minute: first minute, 1 to 3, re-engagements near 3:00 and 6:00, a tolerant back half, an ending never signposted [S1].
5. Paddy Galloway calls it 50/50 (packaging people click, video people watch): plan packaging first; the intro confirms the click without feeling like an intro [S18].
6. Explainers teach better when they state and refute the misconception (effect sizes 0.79 and 0.83 over a clear lecture) [S14].
7. Beats should be joined by "but" or "therefore", not "and then" [S24]; 5 of the 14 pro openings we read already turn on a "but" inside 20 seconds [M].
8. Hooks: clear context, a contrast word, a turn toward the payoff; specifics beat adjectives [S19].
9. Top teams write sound and picture together, redraft about a dozen times, get expert review and re-record lines until they sound right [S15][S16].
10. Template-driven, mass-produced scripts are a monetization risk under YouTube's "inauthentic content" rule (from 2025-07-15) [S10].

## 1. What YouTube itself measures and rewards

- **Signals.** Clicks, watch time, survey answers (1 to 5 star satisfaction ratings behind "valued watch time"), shares, likes and dislikes [S5, P, 2021]; How YouTube Works adds subscriptions and satisfaction surveys [S7, P]. In January 2025 Todd Beaupré (Senior Director, Growth and Discovery) said the system tries to learn how viewers feel about the time spent, and "pulls" videos toward each viewer rather than pushing them out [S6, S].
- **CTR.** Half of channels and videos sit between 2% and 10% impressions CTR. YouTube warns that "clickbait videos tend to have low average view duration" and are then recommended less; the warning pattern is high CTR, low AVD, falling impressions [S3, P]. An impression only counts when a thumbnail is visible for over one second with at least half of it on screen [S12, P].
- **Test & Compare.** Up to 3 titles, thumbnails or combinations, run for up to 2 weeks; the winner is the one with the highest watch time share, because packaging should help viewers know what they are getting [S4, P]. Title testing rolled out globally to creators with advanced features on 2025-12-04 [S4b, S]. During this research one Kurzgesagt upload returned two different titles in two requests minutes apart, consistent with a live title test [M].
- **Retention report.** Intro = % still watching at 0:30; top moments; spikes (rewatched or shared); dips (skipped or left); a flat line means watched straight through, a gradual slope means interest is fading; "typical retention" compares against your 10 latest videos of similar length [S2, P]. YouTube's own advice: rework the first 30 seconds, bring strong moments earlier, study dips [S2].
- **No official benchmarks.** Tables that call 70% at 0:30 solid, or 40% to 55% average viewed good for 10 to 20 minute videos, are community rules of thumb with no published method [S29][S30, S]. Judge against the creator's own typical retention.
- **Chapters:** first timestamp 00:00, at least 3 in ascending order, each at least 10 seconds; automatic chapters are on by default where eligible [S8, P]. **End screens:** only in the last 5 to 20 seconds, video at least 25 seconds, up to 4 elements [S9, P].
- **Inauthentic content (2025-07-15):** repetitive or mass-produced content, including AI content made from generic templates, is not monetizable [S10, P]. Kapwing's November 2025 test found that 21% of the first 500 Shorts shown to a new account were AI-generated [S28, P, vendor study].
- **Trends tab** in YouTube Analytics shows what your audience and viewers across YouTube search for, and content gaps [S11, P].

## 2. Creator and industry playbooks

### 2.1 MrBeast: "How to Succeed in MrBeast Production" (leaked 2024-09) [S1, L]
- Three metrics: CTR, AVD (average view duration), AVP (average percentage viewed).
- **First minute** is the most important: it must match what the thumbnail promised. In one video about 21M of roughly 60M clickers left inside the first minute.
- **Minutes 1 to 3:** "Stop telling people what they will be watching and start showing them." Compress time and jump forward (the doc's *crazy progression*).
- **Near 3:00:** a re-engagement, a spectacle that fits the story. **Minutes 3 to 6:** the simplest, most exciting material, quick scene changes; by 6:00 the viewer should care about the people and the outcome.
- **Near 6:00:** another re-engagement into the back half, where invested viewers tolerate longer explanation (the doc calls it a *lull*).
- **Ending:** never signal the end unless it builds hype for the final reveal; end fast after the payoff.
- **Packaging:** more extreme framing wins (a strong negative claim about bananas beats a mild dislike); a thumbnail detail that does not match the video (a castle of the wrong color) makes viewers feel lied to.
- **Wow factor:** something no other creator could do. **Numbers:** average length 13:37 over 100 videos; one video held viewers 1:38 longer than a similar one and got about 3x the views (120M vs 45M).
- Sponsor reads are woven into the story so skipping loses context [S1c, S]. Caveat: leaked, written for a big-budget challenge channel, not official current practice [S1b]. Take the logic, not the budget.

### 2.2 Paddy Galloway (strategist for MrBeast and many others)
- The job is packaging people click plus a video people watch, and he calls the split 50/50 [S18d, P, 2026-02-17].
- Plan title and thumbnail before recording; get to the value fast; "The best intro doesn't feel like an intro" [S18a, P, 2024-05-10]. Title and thumbnail complement, never repeat; pass the glance test [S18b, P, 2024-07-10].
- The intro confirms the click without overselling; hold attention with re-hooks (new conflict or progress) and foreshadowing; do not spend all the value up front [S18d, P].
- Ideas: test each against Core, Casual and New viewers; funnel ideas from past hits, competitors and fresh thinking; write the top 10 as one-pagers with thumbnail sketch, title and why it works; study outliers for their novelty, their flopped lookalikes and their traffic sources [S18c, P, 2024-09-03]. Familiar plus unexpected; about 80% proven formats, 20% experiments [S18e, S].

### 2.3 Colin and Samir
Three rules: if they don't click, they don't watch; respect their time (the hook pays off the thumbnail inside 30 seconds); make them want more (CTAs, the next idea) [S20, P]. Their interviews are the richest public record of how top creators work (the Rober and Galloway sources here).

### 2.4 Veritasium (Derek Muller)
- **Misconceptions first.** 364 first-year physics students watched one of four versions; the ones that stated and refuted common wrong ideas produced the biggest learning gains (effect sizes 0.79 and 0.83 versus plain exposition) [S14, P, 2008]. A clear but plain explanation can raise confidence without fixing wrong beliefs [S14b, S].
- **Packaging.** In "Clickbait is Unreasonably Effective" (2021-08-17) he treats title and thumbnail as at least half the job, and separates accurate, appealing packaging from packaging that misleads or withholds. A new title and thumbnail took one video projected near 1.5M views to about 14M; a concrete retitle of a Magnus-effect video (backspin basketball off a dam) added about 10M; dropping the word "surprising" lifted a title test by about 10% [S13, P, captions].
- **Openings we measured:** a live test with stakes, or an absurd question, then a "but what if" pivot into a story [M].

### 2.5 Kurzgesagt
1,200+ hours per video, and the script is the bottleneck: weeks to years of books, papers and expert interviews, expert review, about a dozen drafts, a public sources sheet. About 200 illustrated panels; 2 to 3 illustrators for 8 to 12 weeks and 2 to 3 animators for 8 to 10 weeks. The narrator records the whole script, then re-records paragraphs until they feel right [S15, P]. Openings we measured: a contrarian claim or a vivid metaphor, then a "But" turn inside 20 seconds [M].

### 2.6 Johnny Harris and the Vox-style explainer
- Made Vox's Borders (2017 to 2019), independent since 2020, co-founded Newpress in 2026-02 [S16c, S].
- About four months per video. He writes script and visual direction together, with color-coded notes for visual assets and fact-checks; makes a clear promise in title and thumbnail, then widens curiosity past it; principles: visual, plain, clear, concrete; alternates driving prose with reflective pauses; a "there's more going on" approach to story [S16, P episode, S outline, 2025-03-12].
- Vox-style opening we measured: a montage of how the world sees the topic, a "but" question about where that view really comes from, then a number from a named pollster within 20 seconds [M].
- Caveat: critics (McGill's Jonathan Jarry) accuse him of oversimplifying [S16c]. Compression raises accuracy risk; sourcing is part of the craft.

### 2.7 Mark Rober
- Go for a visceral reaction first (amazed, shocked, happy, angry); people do not share videos they did not finish; heart before brain; guide viewers to discover the idea instead of telling them; novelty (something viewers have never seen) wins thumbnails [S17, P, 2026-05-13].
- Hiding the vegetables: the science rides inside entertainment; pack in value, make it matter, be authentic; only 8 to 10 videos a year [S17b, S, talk 2023-01-30]. "Focus on the feeling" [S17c, S, 2025-05-19].
- **"CURIOSITY approach": no primary source found for a named framework or acronym [U].** What his own words support: attention first, then attach the learning.
- Openings we measured: a recent viral incident plus the controversy around it, or a big setup (hundreds of drones, one mission) plus an escalation ("but I'm not stopping there") [M].

### 2.8 Kallaway (hooks)
Three moves [S19, P, 2024-12-19]: **context lean** (make the topic clear and give a reason to lean in), **scroll-stop interjection** (one contrast word: but, however, yet), **contrarian snapback** (a turn against expectation that points at the payoff). Specifics beat adjectives: state a size as a multiple of something familiar instead of calling it huge; no bait and switch. For whole videos: set up, rehook, repeat, and vary sentence length for rhythm [S19b, S].

### 2.9 Ali Abdaal and George Blackman
- Blackman (a YouTube scriptwriter who lists Ali Abdaal among his clients) teaches three levels: 1) structure, 2) setup, tension, payoff per segment, 3) the word-for-word draft; then trim tests: basic, progression and pacing cuts [S21, P].
- Signpost the structure in the hook and at each segment start; he claims 5 seconds of signposting can buy 50 seconds of attention, because "human brains need structure" [S21b, P, 2026-08-07]. Hook shapes: target, transformation, stakes; or proof, promise, plan [S21c, P]. Keep small tension threads alive; make the end-screen CTA a cliffhanger [S21d, P, 2026-08-21].
- Ali's openings we measured name the topic and a method in the first sentence, then add credibility [M]. His own framework could not be read [U].

### 2.10 Dhruv Rathee (India)
Researched Hindi explainers on politics, history and science; 32.4M subscribers on the main channel (2026-05); dubbed channels in five more Indian languages since 2024-04 [S22b, S]. His site offers a research document for each video and published corrections ("Evidence first, belief second") [S22, P]. Lesson: in high-trust explainer markets, visible sources and corrections are part of the product.

### 2.11 Other frameworks worth keeping
- **But/therefore** (Trey Parker and Matt Stone): beats joined by "and then" feel pointless; join them with "but" or "therefore" [S24, P].
- **Title modeling from outliers** (Jake Thomas, Creator Hooks): reuse the structure of titles that beat their channel average (examples at 6.0x to 52.7x), such as versus and authority-plus-fix formats [S23, P, 2026-09-14].
- **30-second blueprint** (1of10, the outlier tool): grab, then viewer gain, then stakes [S29, S, 2025-09-01]; section 3 refines it.

## 3. One promise: title, thumbnail and the first 30 seconds

**Rules.** (1) Title and thumbnail concept come before the script [S18a][S20]. (2) Confirm what was clicked, in the viewer's words, inside about 10 seconds [S1][S18d]. (3) Then escalate: the video must be bigger than the thumbnail [S1]. (4) Title and thumbnail complement, never duplicate [S18b]. (5) Pay the exact promise; YouTube's test rewards watch time, so a clicktrap loses even there [S4].

**30-second intro template** (about 75 to 85 words at 150 to 170 wpm):

| Time | Beat | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 5 s | Confirm | Show or say the exact thing the thumbnail and title promised. |
| 5 to 12 s | Stakes | Why it matters, what is at risk, or what is strange. |
| 12 to 20 s | Turn | A "but" that breaks the obvious answer or names the misconception. |
| 20 to 30 s | Plan or proof | The roadmap, or the first piece of evidence, plus one line of credibility. |

Then cut straight into segment 1. No logo sting, no channel greeting, no subscribe ask.

**Weak vs strong openings** (our own examples; the details are invented for illustration):
- Explainer. Weak: "Have you ever wondered why prices keep rising? Today we'll dive into inflation." Strong: "Ten years ago this basket cost 40 dollars. Today it's 62. The shop's profit barely moved. So who took the 22? By the end, you'll find it on your own receipt."
- Tutorial. Weak: "Hey guys, welcome back! Today I'll show you pivot tables. First, subscribe." Strong: "40,000 rows, a 3 pm meeting, and your boss wants totals by region. It takes three clicks. Then I'll show you the one setting that quietly gives wrong totals."
- Story. Weak: "So I tried living on one dollar a day. Let's see how it goes!" Strong: "One dollar a day, no car, 30 days to reach the coast. Spend a cent more on any day and I start over from zero."
- Commentary. Weak: "There's been a lot of drama lately, so let's talk about it." Strong: "Monday they promised every customer a refund. Thursday the post was gone. I read all 212 pages of the filing, and page 37 changes the story."
- List. Weak: "Here are 10 productivity tips that will change your life." Strong: "I tested 10 famous productivity methods for a week each. Six did nothing. Two made things worse. One now runs my business, and I expected to hate it."

## 4. Retention architecture

- **Open loops.** Keep a ledger: every question raised gets a planned answer point. Close small loops fast (partial payoffs) while the title's question runs toward the end [S18d][S21d].
- **Re-hooks.** Every segment opens with a reason to stay: a new problem, a raised stake, or a promise ("that fixes X, but it breaks Y"). MrBeast schedules big ones near 3:00 and 6:00 [S1]; Kallaway: set up, rehook [S19b]; Blackman adds a one-line signpost [S21b].
- **Pattern interrupts** are a change of mode, not noise: location, graphic, archival clip, test, a person on camera, music or pace. Johnny Harris alternates driving prose with reflective moments [S16]; MrBeast wants quick scene changes in minutes 3 to 6 [S1].
- **Beat density** (our working targets to calibrate against retention data, U): a story turn ("but" or "therefore") every 30 to 60 seconds, a new visual idea every 1 to 3 sentences, a re-hook at every segment start (every 2 to 4 minutes).
- **The mid-video sag.** Curves slope down through the middle; YouTube reads a gradual slope as fading interest [S2]. Practitioners call this "the valley" [U as a named term]. Counter it with a midpoint turn (reversal, new question, or strongest counterargument) placed at about 40% to 55% of runtime [our design rule].
- **Payoff timing.** Confirm early, pay small debts often, pay the main debt late, then stop. Do not spend the best reveal in minute one [S18d]; do not announce the ending [S1].
- **Chapters.** Required format in [S8]. Write chapter titles as mini-promises, not labels ("Why the fix made it worse", not "Part 3"). Whether chapters lift or cut total watch time is not established [U]; tutorials benefit most because viewers jump to steps.
- **CTAs.** No subscribe ask before the intro has paid off [our rule, U as data]. Tie the CTA to the next promise and put it in the end-screen window (last 5 to 20 s) [S9], framed as a cliffhanger [S21d] and without a "that's it for today" wind-down [S1]. Colin and Samir's rule 3 treats CTAs as part of making viewers want more [S20].
- **Sponsors.** Integrate into the story so skipping loses context [S1c]; place after the first payoff, never inside the first 30 seconds [our rule].

## 5. Script mechanics

### 5.1 Speed and length
Reference rates: audiobooks 150 to 160 wpm, slide presentations 100 to 125 wpm [S26]; BBC radio news about 168 wpm [S26b]. Our measurement of words per minute of runtime (includes pauses, music and clips, so pure narration is faster) [M]:

| Channel (recent uploads) | Words per minute of runtime |
|---|---|
| Kurzgesagt (2 videos, about 14 min) | 157 to 167 |
| Johnny Harris (2 videos, 40 to 51 min) | 158 to 171 |
| Veritasium (2 videos, 27 to 36 min) | 185 to 190 |
| Mark Rober (2 videos, 25 to 28 min) | 192 to 197 |
| Vox "America, Actually" (2 videos, 28 to 30 min) | 194 to 206 |
| Ali Abdaal (2 videos, auto captions, heavy jump cuts) | 280 to 291, treat as rough |
| MrBeast | not measurable (caption lines duplicated) |

Planning rule: runtime minutes = words / target wpm (narrated documentary 145 to 165, explainer voice-over 155 to 175, energetic on-camera 175 to 200); a 12-minute explainer at 160 wpm is about 1,900 words. Long-form keeps getting longer: the latest five uploads ran 14 to 15 min (Kurzgesagt), 15 to 58 min (MrBeast), 19 to 34 min (Mark Rober), 23 to 48 min (Veritasium) and 40 min to over three hours (Johnny Harris) [M]. Length should follow the story; YouTube publishes no ideal length.

### 5.2 Writing for the ear (our rules, built on the sources above)
1. One idea per sentence; subject and verb in the first five words.
2. Average 10 to 16 words, with real variance: short punches, a few longer runs, nothing a narrator cannot say in one breath (about 25 words) [S19b].
3. Numbers the way people say them ("about one in five", "nearly double"), one number per sentence, the comparison right next to it.
4. Nothing the ear cannot rewind: no brackets, "the former", "respectively", "as shown above".
5. Repeat the key noun instead of a distant pronoun.
6. Put the surprising word last in the sentence, where the voice lands.
7. Contractions and spoken connectors ("so", "but", "here's the thing"), not "furthermore" or "moreover".
8. Signpost ("Three reasons. First...") [S21b].
9. Show it, then name it: discovery before the label [S17].
10. Read it aloud at target speed; wherever the narrator stumbles or runs out of breath, rewrite [S15][S25].

### 5.3 The A/V two-column script
Left column: narration, dialogue, sound (V.O., SFX). Right column: the matching visual (shot, graphic, map, archival clip, on-screen text). Standard for documentaries, explainers and ads; read it aloud to time it [S25, P, 2025-04-04]. Johnny Harris writes both columns at once; Kurzgesagt builds visual metaphors for the lines [S15][S16]. Right-column rules: concrete and shootable ("close-up: the receipt, total circled", not "b-roll of shopping"); a visual idea every 1 to 3 sentences; every number gets an on-screen form; every abstract idea gets a metaphor or demo.

### 5.4 Cutting and review
- Expect many drafts (Kurzgesagt: about a dozen) [S15]. Trim tests: basic cuts (words and repeats), progression cuts (segments that do not move the story), pacing cuts (slow stretches) [S21]. MrBeast's bar: no dull moments [S1].
- Reviews: packaging one-pager first [S18c]; a cold read by someone who knows nothing; a read-aloud at target speed; expert or fact review [S15]; after release, retention editing: map dips and spikes to script lines and feed the lessons into the next script [S2].

## 6. Format templates

- **Explainer (10 to 14 min).** Hook (confirm, stakes, turn, plan) > the common belief and why it fails (misconception) [S14] > mechanism 1, shown before named > midpoint turn (the complication) > mechanism 2 or human case > strongest counterargument > payoff that answers the title > one-line implication > end screen.
- **Documentary or video essay (15 to 30 min).** Cold open in a scene > thesis question by about 1:00 > the world before > inciting event > complications joined by but/therefore [S24] > a re-engagement every few minutes (MrBeast plans them near 3:00 and 6:00) [S1] > midpoint reversal > climax and answer > what it means now.
- **Story-driven challenge (12 to 20 min).** Rules, stakes and end state in the first minute, delivered bigger than the thumbnail > fast progression > spectacle near 3:00 > simple high-energy beats > re-engagement near 6:00 > character depth in the back half > climax > abrupt end [S1].
- **Tutorial (6 to 15 min).** Finished result on screen, who it is for, time needed > the trap most people fall into > steps (goal, action, check), one chapter each [S8] > the mistakes section > result plus the next tutorial.
- **Commentary (8 to 20 min).** Strongest piece of evidence first > your claim in one sentence > 60 seconds of context (who, what, when) > evidence ladder with receipts > the best version of the other side > verdict and what to watch next.
- **List video (10 to 15 min).** Hook with the ranking logic and stakes > items in escalating order with #1 held as the open loop > each item: claim, proof, twist, micro-payoff > vary item length and format so it never feels like a loop > #1 pays the title.

## 7. Research-to-script workflow (how pros actually work)

1. **Demand:** Trends tab searches and content gaps [S11]; outliers in your niche and adjacent ones [S18c][S23]; comments on those outliers for questions, confusions and objections [our method].
2. **Filter:** Core, Casual, New test; familiar plus unexpected; can you draw the thumbnail today? [S18a][S18c][S18e].
3. **Angle:** one-sentence promise plus the misconception or tension it breaks [S14]; look for the "there's more going on" layer [S16].
4. **Packaging:** 3 titles and 3 thumbnail concepts ready for Test & Compare [S4].
5. **Research doc:** primary sources, dated numbers, expert check, a publishable source sheet [S15][S22].
6. **Structure (level 1):** segments, the open-loop ledger, payoff placement [S21].
7. **Beats (level 2):** setup, tension, payoff per segment, joined by but/therefore [S21][S24].
8. **Draft (level 3)** in A/V columns [S16][S25].
9. **Edit passes:** promise, clarity, trim tests, ear, fact, slop.
10. **Learn:** retention key moments against typical retention [S2]; packaging tests [S4].

## 8. Failure modes and "AI slop" tells

In the 14 pro transcripts we scanned, "let's dive in" appeared zero times and "dive into" once. Plain "in this video" did appear (Ali Abdaal opens one video with it), but it introduced a concrete, named promise, not filler [M]. So the tell is not one phrase: it is **throat-clearing in place of a hook**, plus these patterns:

- **Openers:** "In today's video", "Hey guys, welcome back", "Have you ever wondered", "Let's dive in", "Buckle up", "In a world where", "Without further ado".
- **Hype without specifics:** "game-changer", "mind-blowing", "you won't believe" with no number, name, date or scene behind it.
- **LLM vocabulary and syntax** listed by Wikipedia's AI Cleanup guide: delve, tapestry, testament, pivotal, intricate, underscore; "serves as" instead of "is"; "not just X, but Y"; reflexive groups of three; vague "experts say" attributions; excessive em dashes [S27].
- **Listicle monotony:** every segment has the same shape and length, no escalation, no reason for the order.
- **No stakes, no POV:** both-sides fog in commentary, nobody at risk in a story.
- **Recap endings:** "In conclusion", "Ultimately", a moral, then "thanks for watching".
- **Generic visual notes:** "b-roll of city", "stock footage of people typing".
- **Uniform rhythm:** every sentence 15 to 20 words, every paragraph three sentences, constant enthusiasm.

How pros avoid them: start in a scene or a claim, name real specifics, give the creator a stated opinion, join beats causally, vary rhythm, and let visuals carry proof.

## 9. Implications for our skill

### 9.1 Hard rules
1. Packaging first: every script starts with a promise contract (below) and 3 title plus 3 thumbnail options.
2. Topic confirmation within the first 25 words; a turn ("but", "except", "yet") or a hard stake within the first 20 seconds.
3. No greeting, logo, subscribe ask or sponsor in the first 30 seconds.
4. Every title question is paid off explicitly, after the midpoint, with partial payoffs before it.
5. Beats are joined by but/therefore; "and then" chains are rewritten.
6. Each segment opens with a re-hook and a one-line signpost.
7. A midpoint turn at 40% to 55% of runtime; for videos over 8 minutes, a re-engagement at least every 3 to 4 minutes.
8. Explainers state the common misconception and refute it.
9. Every script is written in A/V columns with a concrete visual for each 1 to 3 sentences.
10. Every factual claim carries a source line; a source sheet ships with the script.
11. The end is the payoff, then one CTA tied to the next video in the last 5 to 20 seconds; no wind-down.
12. Zero banned phrases; no em or spaced en dashes.

### 9.2 Automatic checks (lint)
- Intro word count to the "plan" beat: 60 to 90 words.
- Runtime estimate = words / chosen wpm (defaults: documentary 150, explainer 160, energetic 185); flag if more than 10% off the target length.
- Sentence length: median 9 to 16 words; at most 10% over 25 words; at least 15% at 8 words or fewer.
- "And then" count; contrast and causal connector count per minute (flag stretches over 90 seconds with none).
- Open-loop ledger: every opened question has a closing line reference.
- Specifics density: at least one number, name, date or place per 100 words in explainers and commentary.
- Banned phrase and AI-vocabulary list [S27]; repeated segment shapes in list videos.
- Visual column: no empty cells, no generic "b-roll" notes.

### 9.3 Templates to ship
- **Promise contract:** viewer, title, thumbnail concept, the one-sentence promise, first spoken line, first visual, the misconception or tension, where the main payoff lands (timestamp), what makes it bigger than the thumbnail.
- **Intro 0 to 30 s:** confirm, stakes, turn, plan or proof (section 3).
- **Segment block:** re-hook line, signpost, setup, tension, payoff, bridge ("but" or "therefore" into the next).
- **Beat maps** for the six formats (section 6), with word budgets from the wpm rule.
- **A/V table:** time, narration, visual, on-screen text, sound, source.

### 9.4 Judge rubric (score 1 to 5 each; weights sum to 100)

| Criterion | Weight | 5 looks like | 1 looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Promise alignment | 15 | Title, thumbnail, first 30 s and payoff are one promise; delivers more than promised | Intro or payoff does not match the package |
| Hook | 10 | Specific, stakes, turn, plan in under 90 words | Greeting, generic question, slow context |
| Narrative engine | 15 | But/therefore chain, loops opened and closed, midpoint turn, rising stakes | "And then" sequence, flat middle |
| Clarity and learning | 10 | Misconception refuted, signposted, jargon shown before named | Lecture tone, lost viewer |
| Specificity and evidence | 10 | Dated numbers, names, places, sources on every claim | Vague "experts say", no sources |
| Ear quality | 10 | Reads aloud cleanly, varied rhythm, speakable numbers | Written-for-eye sentences, breathless clauses |
| Visual writing | 10 | Concrete visual per line, metaphors, show before tell | Empty or generic visual notes |
| Voice and originality | 10 | Clear POV, lines only this creator would say, zero slop tells | Template feel, AI vocabulary |
| Ending and CTA | 5 | Ends on the payoff, one CTA to the next promise | Recap, moral, "thanks for watching" drag |
| Length discipline | 5 | Every segment earns its minutes | Padding, repeated points |

**Hard fails** (score 0 regardless of total): package and payoff mismatch; an unsourced or invented key fact; a banned phrase in the first 30 seconds; the title question never answered.

**Judge output:** the total, a retention risk map (timestamps where viewers are likely to leave, and why), the three highest-value fixes with line references, and pass or fail. Judge blind (script, package and audience only, never the writer's notes) and read at the target wpm.
