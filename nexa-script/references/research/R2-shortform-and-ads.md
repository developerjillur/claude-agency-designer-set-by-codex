# R2. Short-form video and video ad scripts (2024 to 2026)

Research file for the script-writing skill. Compiled 2026-09-26. Scope: YouTube Shorts, Instagram and Facebook Reels, TikTok, paid social video, YouTube ads, UGC-style ads, direct-response and brand ads.

How to read it: [S#] points to the source list at the end (publisher, title, URL, date). "Secondary" means a third party reporting a primary source I could not load; "unverified" means no primary confirmation; "heuristic" marks our own working rule. Popular vendor-blog numbers with no stated method (a "60% sound-on rate" for Reels, "4x distribution" for low Shorts swipe-away) are left out on purpose.

## 1. What the platforms say and measure

### 1.1 TikTok

- **Structure is hook, body, close.** TikTok's Creative Codes say the first six seconds carry most of the effect: 90% of ad recall impact and 80% of awareness impact land within 6 seconds (TikTok "Value of View" analysis, 2020) [S2]. The ads help page asks for the content proposition in the first 3 seconds and the hook inside the first 6 [S1].
- **Six Creative Codes:** TikTok First (lo-fi, 9:16, real people), Structure, Stimulation (music, transitions, movement, text pop-ups), Sound, Trends, Production (high resolution, clear of the UI) [S2].
- **Native beats polished.** TikTok-first ads drove close to 3.3x more actions such as clicks and shares (Marketcast, 2022); remaking TV-style assets as TikTok-first lifted intent 37% and favorability 38% (Kantar, 3,500 ads, 2022) [S2].
- **Hook styles:** early suspense +16% watch time, surprise 1.7x view-through rate, an emotional opening 1.7x awareness lift (Lumen, 2021) [S2].
- **Sound:** 73% of users would stop and look at a sound-on TikTok ad; about 9 in 10 call sound vital (Kantar, 2021) [S2][S4].
- **Conversion attributes** (machine-learning study, Dec 2021) [S3]: 21 to 34 s length +280% conversion lift; 720p+ +312%; 9:16 +91%; text CTA +152%; e-commerce: several scenes +38%, captions or on-screen text +80%, voiceover plus offer +87%; gaming: 5+ scenes +171%, text in the first 7 s +43%. These are 2021 correlations, so treat them as direction only.
- **Other effects:** product on screen +65% brand affinity and +25% recall; CTA cards +45% recall [S4].
- **Text density:** "We recommend displaying 5-10 words per second when using text." [S1]. That only fits single words popping on the beat (see 4.3).
- **Testing and specs:** 3 to 5 clearly different creatives per ad group, refreshed when daily new users fall [S1]; 9:16; non-Spark ads up to 10 minutes, Spark Ads (boosted organic posts) unlimited; official safe-zone templates [S5].

### 1.2 Meta: Facebook and Instagram Reels

- **Meta's Reels ad page:** 9:16 video with audio and key messages in the safe zone had 34.5% lower cost per result than image ads (meta-analysis of 15 split tests) and 2x more Reels delivery; adding partner (creator) native Reels cut cost per result 5% and lifted conversion rate 11%. Reels play sound on by default [S9].
- **Oct 2023:** 9:16 video with sound had 4.8% lower CPA and 5.1% higher CTR than other video; music plus voiceover scored 15 points higher on positive response [S10].
- **Hooks (Meta Marketing blog, Dec 2025, reported by Social Media Today):** "Great Reels nail the hook within the first few seconds." Meta names three hook types (value promise, statement of intent, question or invitation), tells advertisers to A/B test hooks, and says music or voiceover delivered up to 13% more incremental conversions [S11].
- **Creative diversification (Dec 16, 2025):** distinct assets for different personas or use cases, varying visual style, message and CTA, format, motivation, and emotional story versus product features [S12]. The driver is Andromeda, the 2024 retrieval engine that picks from tens of millions of candidate ads; over a million advertisers made more than 15 million GenAI ads in one month [S13]. Vendor claims about hidden "Entity IDs" and a 60% similarity cutoff are unverified [S41].
- **Attention speed:** people recall mobile feed content after 0.25 seconds of exposure and spend about 1.7 seconds per feed item on mobile (Facebook IQ, 2016 data) [S15].
- **Reels ranking signals (Meta Transparency Center, Nov 2025):** predictions of whether a viewer skips in the first seconds, watches nearly to the end (judged against Reels of similar length), likes, comments, reshares in the app or off it, uses the audio, and follows [S14].
- **Originality:** from Apr 30, 2026, accounts that mostly repost other people's work are no longer recommended to non-followers; watermarks and small crops do not count as transformation [S17].
- **Safe zone:** in 2026 Meta unified the 9:16 safe zone for Stories and Reels: keep text, logos and CTAs out of the top 14%, 6% on each side and the bottom 20% to 35% [S16, secondary: Meta's own help page did not render].

### 1.3 YouTube Shorts and YouTube ads

- Shorts can run to 3 minutes (square or vertical) since Oct 15, 2024 [S21][S22]. Most songs are usable for up to 90 seconds, some only 30 or 60; from Sept 24, 2026, Shorts over 1 minute with Content ID claims are no longer blocked automatically [S21].
- From Mar 31, 2025, a Shorts view counts every start or replay; the old measure survives as "engaged views" and still decides monetization [S23]. Judge Shorts by engaged views, average view duration and the viewed vs swiped away split in YouTube Studio [S24].
- **Shorts ads:** winners feel like part of the feed: creator-made, casual, everyday people talking to camera, upbeat and worth sharing (Apr 2024) [S25]. Vertical assets gave 10% to 20% more conversions per dollar on Shorts than landscape alone; there is no time to set up a premise, and viewers expect emotion (Sept 2022) [S26].
- **Google experiments (Feb 2024):** a 49-second Spider-Verse interview Short did as well as or better than the 15-second trailer; a direct-to-camera vertical Dior cut lifted recall and search; creator-led Pixel ads gave 49% higher watch time, 128% higher consideration and 56% lower cost per lifted user than the control [S27].

### 1.4 Google's ABCD framework

Google built the ABCDs with Ipsos, with Nielsen and Kantar reviewing. Ads following them showed on average a 30% lift in short-term sales likelihood and a 17% lift in long-term brand contribution (Google/Kantar Link AI, 2021) [S28][S29].

| Letter | Core guideline [S28] | Tuned by objective [S29] |
|---|---|---|
| Attention | Jump in with tight framing; back the story with audio and supers; bright, high-contrast visuals | Awareness: lean on voice, music and sound effects |
| Branding | Brand early and throughout; say the brand in audio; use every brand asset | Awareness: frequent and varied brand cues. Consideration: hero the product, keep branding up in the last 5 seconds. Action: product on screen start to finish, extreme close-ups |
| Connection | Put people in the story; one focused message; humor, surprise, intrigue | Consideration: show the product in real life. Action: a precise, credible use case |
| Direction | Clear CTA; say it as well as show it | Consideration: add urgency (deadline, limited release). Action: tie the CTA to an offer or discount |

TikTok asks that branding inside the hook stay subtle [S2]; Google asks for it early. The script rule that satisfies both: show the product or a brand cue inside the first 3 to 5 seconds as part of the action, never as a logo slate.

### 1.5 Industry metrics and benchmarks

| Metric | Definition | Benchmarks with credible sourcing |
|---|---|---|
| Hook rate (thumb-stop ratio) | 3-second video plays / impressions [S34] | Motion calls 20% to 40% solid, varying by industry [S34]. Billo, 88,329 Meta sales video ads, Jan to Jun 2026: average rose from 24.42% to 25.44% [S40]. Pilothouse targets 30% to 40% [S42] |
| Hold rate | ThruPlays / 3-second plays; a ThruPlay is 15 seconds or completion [S35] | No neutral benchmark; Pilothouse targets 25%+ from 3 to 15 seconds [S42] |
| TikTok hold | share who watch 6 seconds or the whole video [S36] | Compare with account baseline |
| Shorts viewed vs swiped away | feed viewers who stay vs swipe [S24] | No official benchmark |
| Sends, saves, comments, follows | Reels ranking predictions [S14] | Compare with account averages, as Motion advises [S36] |
| Winner rate | ad spend at least 10x the account median and at least $500 | About 5% of creatives; 55% of all spend goes to winners (578,750 creatives, 6,015 accounts, Sept 2025 to Jan 2026) [S37] |

Hook rate is a diagnostic, not the goal. In Billo's data Toys and Games had the best hook rate (28.79%) but ranked 12th of 14 on ROAS, while Baby and Toddler had the lowest CTR and the best ROAS [S40].

## 2. Hooks in the first 1 to 3 seconds

A hook has three layers carrying one idea: first frame (visual), first spoken line (verbal) and overlay (text). Google's "jump in", TikTok's proposition in 3 seconds and Meta's hook types agree [S1][S11][S28]. Pilothouse adds a face and the product or brand name in the opening frames, and prefers "recognition" hooks that mirror the viewer's situation over shock [S42][S45]. Foreplay mines the competitor hooks that have run longest, since longevity signals they sell [S49].

Hook types, with my own examples:

| Type | Good | Weak |
|---|---|---|
| Result first | "Five lunches, 40 minutes, one Sunday." (pan across the packed fridge) | "Meal prep can be hard, but it doesn't have to be!" |
| Value promise | "Three settings that get your phone to bedtime on one charge." | "Some useful phone tips." |
| Statement of intent | "I'm fixing this wobbly table with one thing from the kitchen drawer. Watch." | "In this video I'll talk about furniture." |
| Question or invitation | "Guess what this whole outfit cost." | "Have you ever thought about fashion?" |
| Proof shot | Phone dropped in a fish tank at frame 1, text: Waterproof? Let's see. | Slow logo reveal, then a model smiling |
| Mistake or warning | "Stop rinsing rice like this." | "Rice is eaten by billions of people." |
| In medias res | "11 pm, my landlord texts: is that your car?" | "Let me tell you a story." |
| Comparison | Split screen, powder clumps, ours dissolves: "Same water, ten seconds." | "Unlike other brands, we're better." |
| Recognition or POV | Text: POV: black couch, golden retriever. | "Everyone deals with pet hair." |
| Objection first | "Yes, it's $60 for socks. Here's why I bought four pairs." | "Premium socks for everyone." |
| Offer | "Two for one ends Sunday. Here's what's in the box." | "Amazing deals now!" |

Hook rules:
1. Something happens in frame 1 and speech starts within about half a second. No greeting, no "hey guys", no logo slate.
2. One idea per hook. The overlay is 7 words or fewer and restates the promise for sound-off viewers.
3. The body must pay the hook off; a gap shows up as a weak hold rate [S35] and can breach clickbait rules [S32].
4. Never call out a sensitive trait of the viewer (see 6.2). "Struggling with your weight?" invites rejection under Meta's personal-attribute and appearance rules; "What I eat on a 12-hour shift so I'm not starving at 4 pm" does not [S18][S19].

## 3. Structures and beat templates

Timings assume speech at about 2.5 words per second (see 4.1). Seconds are targets.

### 3.1 Organic shorts

- **Loop (7 to 15 s).** Open mid-sentence on the payoff image, build, and end on a line that runs into the first one: "so now I pack this one pouch" loops into "that saved my whole Lisbon trip." No end card, no "follow for more". Replays now count as Shorts views and Reels ranking predicts near-complete watching [S14][S23].
- **List or "3 things" (20 to 35 s).** 0 to 2 s: number plus stakes ("3 things I'd never buy again as a baker"). Each item 6 to 8 s: name on screen, one line of why, one visual proof. Best item last, then an open question for comments.
- **Story (30 to 60 s).** Cold open at the tensest moment, one line of context, two escalations, a turn, the payoff, a last line people want to send. TikTok lists storytime among evergreen formats [S2].
- **Tutorial (20 to 45 s).** Result in the first 2 s, numbered steps of 6 s at most, the common mistake, the result again, a save prompt.
- **POV (7 to 20 s).** Setup in on-screen text, an acted reaction, little voiceover, licensed audio.
- **Green-screen commentary (20 to 45 s).** A screenshot, review or chart behind the creator; the hook reacts to it; two or three points; a clear opinion. TikTok names green screen and duets as native tools [S4].

### 3.2 UGC and performance ad structures

Motion treats structures such as before and after, testimonial, founder rant and demo as concepts, and hooks, colors or opening clips as variants [S39]. TikTok suggests a-ha moments, problem and solution, and customer-journey narratives [S2]. They can run from the creator's handle as Spark Ads on TikTok or partner creative on Meta [S5][S9].

| Structure | Beat plan (30 s unless noted) |
|---|---|
| Problem, agitate, solution | 0 to 3 problem shown; 3 to 8 the specific cost of it; 8 to 20 product solving it on camera; 20 to 26 proof (number, review, result); 26 to 30 CTA with offer |
| Testimonial | 0 to 3 result in the creator's own words; 3 to 10 life before; 10 to 20 product in use plus one surprising detail; 20 to 26 one doubt answered; 26 to 30 recommendation and CTA |
| Founder story (30 to 45 s) | 0 to 3 the moment it started; 3 to 12 the problem; 12 to 25 what they built and the one insight; 25 to 35 proof; last 5 s an invitation |
| Before and after (15 to 20 s) | 0 to 2 the after; 2 to 8 the before, dated; 8 to 14 the process with the product; 14 to 20 CTA. Check policy first (6.3) |
| Demo | 0 to 2 a test that looks impossible; 2 to 15 one unbroken take; 15 to 22 the one mechanism behind it; 22 to 30 CTA |
| Reasons why | Hook "3 reasons I switched"; three reasons of about 7 s, each with its own shot; CTA. Foreplay credits a "3 Reasons Why" UGC concept for ARMRA [S50] |
| Objection handling | 0 to 3 state the objection bluntly; 3 to 20 answer it with proof; 20 to 30 CTA with risk reversal (trial, guarantee) |
| Us vs them | 0 to 3 side-by-side test; 3 to 18 two or three substantiated differences; 18 to 25 verdict; 25 to 30 CTA. Comparative claims must be truthful and backed [S63] |

Evidence for the style: Common Thread Collective says iPhone-style UGC Reels are its best ads in 90% of its accounts [S52]; Motion found 42% of top-spending ads were lo-fi [S38]; Foreplay reports ARMRA's UGC at a $175.90 CPA on $1.8M spend vs $182.45 for traditional video (vendor figures, unaudited) [S50].

### 3.3 Direct-response video: the Harmon Brothers approach

- **Squatty Potty (2015 unicorn ad):** about $200,000 production plus $50,000 seeding, built on "a little bit of humor, a little bit of education, and then a sell"; 500 million+ views and $38 million of 2016 sales are reported [S54]; 2015 revenue was $18.7 million [S55]. The repeated "600% online sales lift" is unverified.
- **Purple (2016):** the Goldilocks raw-egg test showed the product instead of describing it [S57]; about 40 videos averaging near 2 minutes viewed, many tested in 20+ versions, $75 million online sales in 2016 [S56]. In 2024 Harmon message-tested with still ads on Meta before scripting, then ran a 2:39 hero film plus 10 spots; 38% of YouTube viewers watched 30+ seconds [S58].
- **Poo-Pourri, "Girls Don't Poop" (2013):** one of the brands covered in the 2018 book on Harmon Brothers [S68]; the ad passed 31 million views and the brand reached 9,000 stores within months [S60].
- **Method:** name the problem nobody talks about, make the product the answer, add humor, keep a CTA [S59]. Harmon rejects fixed 15/30/60 lengths: the ad runs as long as the sale needs, because "Sales is the core goal" [S53].

Long-form DR template (90 to 160 s): 0 to 10 funny cold open on the problem; 10 to 20 character and product; 20 to 50 education (why the problem exists); 50 to 80 demonstration; 80 to 110 proof; 110 to 130 objections; 130 to 150 offer, guarantee, CTA; last 10 s a callback joke and the CTA again. Cut 6, 15 and 30 s versions from the same footage.

### 3.4 Brand story ads

Use the Awareness ABCDs: people at the center, sound doing work, brand cues repeated in varied forms [S29]. Humor is underused: 25% of ads spending $1M+ used humor vs 14% overall, and Motion cites Kantar finding humor in 33% of ads but 50% of effective ones [S38]. Template (30 to 60 s): a human moment (0 to 5); tension (5 to 20); the brand as enabler, not hero (20 to 40); emotional payoff (40 to 50); sign-off with a sonic or visual brand asset (50 to 60).

## 4. Pacing, length, text and sound

### 4.1 Words per second

US conversational English runs about 150 words per minute, 2.5 per second (National Center for Voice and Speech); five popular TED talks averaged 173 wpm [S65]. Heuristic budgets at 2.3 to 2.8 words per second, leaving room for visual beats:

| Length | Spoken words |
|---|---|
| 6 s bumper | 12 to 15 |
| 15 s | 30 to 40 |
| 30 s | 65 to 80 |
| 45 s | 100 to 120 |
| 60 s | 135 to 160 |
| 90 s | 200 to 240 |

Energetic UGC can reach 3 words per second in the hook only. For Bangla and other languages, time the voiceover with a read-aloud or TTS pass instead of counting words.

### 4.2 Length and cuts

- Length: 21 to 34 s carried TikTok's largest conversion lift in 2021 [S3]; on Shorts a 49 s cut matched or beat 15 s [S27]; long-form DR runs as long as the sale needs [S53], and Motion notes educational ads that bring the product in after 40 to 50 seconds [S38].
- Cuts: more scenes lifted conversion (38% e-commerce, 171% gaming for 5+) [S3] and fast scene changes pull viewers in early [S2]. Heuristic: a new shot, angle, zoom or text change every 1.5 to 3 s in the first 6 s, then every 2 to 4 s; 6+ distinct shots in a 30 s ad.

### 4.3 Captions, on-screen text and sound

- Design for both states. TikTok builds its guidance around sound-on viewing [S2], Reels play sound by default [S9] and Google describes YouTube as close to fully sound-on [S29], yet in the 2019 Verizon Media and Publicis Media study (5,616 US adults) 80% said they are more likely to finish a video when captions are available [S66].
- Readability: Netflix caps adult subtitles at 20 characters per second, 42 characters per line and two lines [S67]. TikTok's 5 to 10 words per second only fits single words popping on the beat [S1].
- Rules: burned captions follow the voice in chunks of 1 to 4 words; hook overlay 7 words or fewer and on screen at least 1.5 s; one text zone at a time.

### 4.4 Safe zones

Meta: top 14%, sides 6%, bottom up to 35% [S16]. TikTok: use the official templates [S5]; vendor guides put the right icon column at roughly 100 to 160 px and the bottom caption band at roughly 270 to 370 px on 1080x1920 (unverified). Shorts has a similar right-side button column and bottom title band (unverified). Heuristic master box for one asset on all three: critical text and logos between 14% and 65% of the height and between 8% and 86% of the width.

### 4.5 CTA wording and placement

Text CTAs lifted conversions 152% in TikTok's 2021 study [S3]; CTA cards added 45% recall [S4]; Google says to say the CTA as well as show it, give a reason to act now and tie it to the offer [S28][S29]. Write the CTA as verb plus object plus reason: "Tap Shop Now, the starter kit is 20% off until Sunday." Match the button label. Put the final CTA in the last 3 to 5 s, add a soft mid-roll CTA in ads of 30 s or more (heuristic), and keep CTA text above the bottom 35%.

## 5. Creative testing and iteration

- **Concept first, then variants.** Motion's classic advice is to test concepts and iterate on winners, while noting Andromeda has weakened small iterations [S39]. Meta wants distinct personas and use cases [S12]; Pilothouse warns that button colors or one-word edits are not variation and recommends 8 to 12 distinct concepts per campaign, refreshed every 7 to 14 days [S48].
- **Generate concepts systematically.** Pilothouse's P.D.A. grid crosses persona, desire and angle (5 x 4 x 6 gives 120 concepts) [S44]; its Creative Triangle aims for 20+ different angles per cycle across cold, mid-funnel and customer layers [S46]; its 3-3-3 grid crosses 3 funnel stages, 3 angles and 3 formats [S47].
- **Hook testing.** 3 to 5 hooks on one fixed body (10 to 15+ later), varying the first frame and the person on screen; run 48 to 72 hours and at least 1,000 impressions each, ideally 10,000+ [S42].
- **Volume.** Enterprise accounts launch about 18.8 creatives a week; more volume gives more chances at a winner, while the odds per ad stay flat [S37]. Motion's 2025 report says top DTC brands make 50 to 70 new ads a week [S38].
- **Fatigue.** Thumb-stop rate often drops 3 to 7 days before downstream metrics, alongside rising CPMs [S48]; TikTok says refresh when daily new users fall [S1].
- **Test messages before production,** as Harmon did with still ads for Purple [S58]; Purple's earlier rule was to spend about $100 per test and re-cut [S56].
- **Modular script.** Hooks H1 to H5 (different types and first frames, not synonyms), bodies B1 and B2, CTAs C1 and C2, each block ending on a clean cut so any hook joins any body.

**Briefing UGC creators.** Pilothouse argues for outcome-based briefs: objective, a mindset persona, the intent being resolved, 2 or 3 narrative directions, a visual hook direction, value props in plain speech, energy references, guardrails, specs and timeline; word-for-word scripts create an "uncanny valley" that looks like a testimonial and sounds like an ad [S43]. TikTok: give creators a clear brief and trust their style [S2]. Foreplay: give examples and constraints and make execution easy [S51]. Always add must-say claims with proof, forbidden claims, the disclosure rule (6.4) and the usage rights needed.

Teams covered: Motion, Foreplay, Pilothouse and Common Thread Collective (cited above). Thesis's site did not load, so nothing is attributed to it.

## 6. Rules and restrictions

1. **Claims.** Ads must be truthful and the advertiser needs evidence before making a claim, implied claims included [S63]. TikTok rejects exaggerated results, such as a lotion promising instantly slimmer legs or a finance product promising money in seconds [S7]. Google bans improbable results like get-rich-quick schemes or miracle cures [S32].
2. **Personal attributes (Meta).** Ads may not assert or imply race, religion, age, sexual orientation, gender identity, disability, health, financial vulnerability, voting status, criminal record and similar traits: no questions about the viewer's condition, no "Meet other seniors". Describing the product, or using "you" without a trait, is fine [S18].
3. **Health, weight, cosmetics.** Meta: no claims to cure conditions like diabetes or cancer; weight-loss ads target 18+, no fat-pinching close-ups, no attacks on appearance; before-and-after is allowed for cosmetic products and procedures targeted to 18+ [S19]. TikTok rejects before-and-after comparisons that could give a false impression [S7]. Google restricts drug terms and needs certification for most healthcare ads [S33].
4. **Endorsements.** Disclose paid ties inside the video, early, spoken and on screen; endorsers must have used the product; show typical results when a result is unusual [S62]. The FTC's 2024 rule bans fake and AI-generated reviews and testimonials, including from people who do not exist [S61].
5. **Music.** TikTok business accounts must use the Commercial Music Library (about 1 million pre-cleared songs) for organic and paid posts [S6]. YouTube limits song length in Shorts and applies Content ID [S21]. Never script a trending song into an ad without a license.
6. **AI disclosure.** TikTok ads with significantly AI-modified content need the AIGC label or a clear disclaimer, caption, watermark or sticker, or they are rejected; fake public-figure endorsements are banned [S7]. TikTok also reads C2PA credentials to auto-label [S8]. YouTube requires disclosure of realistic synthetic people, places or events, but not for AI help with scripts [S30]. Meta adds "AI info" labels and requires disclosure on political or social issue ads [S20]; Google requires it on election ads [S31]. In the EU, Article 50 of the AI Act requires deepfakes to be disclosed from Aug 2, 2026 [S64].

## 7. Common failures and AI tells

Failures:
- Slow starts: greetings, logo slates, context before conflict [S26].
- A hook the body never pays off [S32][S35].
- Three messages in 30 seconds [S28].
- Features without a demo; no product until second 20.
- A script for the ear only (no visual directions) or the eye only (no voiceover).
- Text inside UI zones, or longer than it can be read.
- TV polish on TikTok or Reels [S2].
- Cosmetic variants sold as a test plan [S48].
- Chasing hook rate while ROAS falls [S40].
- Unlicensed trending music; reposted content [S6][S17].

AI tells to ban in short scripts:
- Stock openers: "Are you tired of", "Picture this", "In today's fast-paced world", "Let's dive in", "Say goodbye to X and hello to Y", "Meet X, your new".
- Hype words: game-changer, revolutionary, unlock, elevate, seamless, effortless, level up, must-have.
- Rhythm: a list of three in every sentence, equal sentence lengths, the "The result? X." reveal, a question answered at once every time, no fragments or natural fillers in creator speech.
- Content: no numbers, times, places or sensory detail; invented testimonials or stats; abstract payoffs like "confidence" with no concrete moment.
- Endings: a summary line plus "So what are you waiting for?".
- Voice: brand phrases in a creator's mouth ("our innovative formula"), nonstop positivity, no admitted downside.

Weak vs good, 30 s UGC ad for a fictional pet-hair tool.

Weak (23 words, generic, no visual): "Tired of pet hair everywhere? Say goodbye to messy couches and hello to FurAway, the revolutionary tool that makes cleaning effortless. Shop now!"

Good (64 words, about 2.1 per second; demo-heavy ads can sit under the budget):

| Time | Visual | Voiceover | Text |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3 | One swipe cuts a clean stripe through fur on a black couch | "One swipe. That's a whole week of Biscuit." | 1 swipe vs 1 week of dog hair |
| 3 to 8 | Creator on the couch, dog climbs up | "Since we got him, I go through three lint rollers a month." | |
| 8 to 17 | Quick cuts: couch, car seat, black jeans | "This has no sheets. Rub it back and forth, the hair piles up in here, tip it in the bin." | No refills |
| 17 to 22 | Opens the chamber, dumps a fur ball | "Look at that. That was just the back seat." | |
| 22 to 26 | Rinses it under the tap | "When it gets gross, I rinse it." | |
| 26 to 30 | Holds it up, dog in frame | "It's 30% off this week. Link's right there." | 30% off until Sunday |

## 8. Implications for our skill

### Rules
1. Before writing, pin down: platform and placement, objective (awareness, consideration, action), length, audience, the proof available, the offer, allowed claims, voice (creator, founder, brand) and language.
2. Output four fields per beat: time, visual, voiceover, on-screen text, plus sound notes. Every beat needs a visual.
3. Hook: action in frame 1, first word by 0.5 s, promise clear by 3 s, overlay of 7 words or fewer, product or brand cue by 3 to 5 s without a logo slate.
4. Stay inside the word budget in 4.1; flag any spoken sentence over 18 words.
5. One message per ad; proof before the CTA; CTA spoken and on screen as verb plus object plus reason.
6. Respect the safe box: nothing critical in the top 14%, bottom 35%, right 14% or left 8%.
7. Ads ship as a modular pack: 5 hooks of at least 4 types, 2 bodies, 2 CTAs, and which combinations to test first.
8. UGC jobs ship a brief as well as a sample script: outcome, persona, 2 or 3 story directions, must-say and must-not-say, disclosure, specs.
9. Never invent testimonials, stats, reviews or results; tag every claim that needs evidence with [PROOF NEEDED].
10. Music notes name licensed sources only; add an AI disclosure note whenever the plan uses synthetic people, voices or scenes.

### Templates to ship
Loop, list, story, tutorial, POV, green screen (3.1); problem-agitate-solution, testimonial, founder, before and after, demo, reasons why, objection, us vs them (3.2); long-form DR with cutdowns (3.3); brand story (3.4). Each stores beats in seconds, a word budget and the required shots.

### Automatic checks
- Words per second per beat and in total against the budget.
- First second: speech or a described action between 0 and 1 s; no greeting words.
- Product or brand visible by 5 s.
- Personal-attribute patterns: "Are you", "Do you have", "your [condition]", "other [group]" next to a sensitive trait.
- Risk words: cure, heal, guaranteed, best, #1, clinically proven, risk-free, instant, overnight, money amounts with time frames; before-and-after in health, weight or finance.
- The AI-tell list in section 7; no em or en dashes.
- CTA present, specific and spoken; a disclosure line when the creator is paid.
- Hook pack diversity: at least 4 hook types and 3 different first frames across 5 hooks.
- Overlays of 7 words or fewer; captions in 1 to 4 word chunks at 20 characters per second at most.

### Judge criteria (1 to 5 each; ship at an average of 4+ with no score under 3)
1. Stop power: would a stranger stop within a second, sound off? Is it specific?
2. Promise and payoff: does the body pay off the hook within 5 to 8 s?
3. Product and proof: is the product shown working, with a concrete, provable detail?
4. Voice: does it sound like a real creator on that platform, free of AI tells?
5. Sound-off and sound-on: does the story work both ways?
6. Platform fit: length, pace, safe zone, native format.
7. CTA: clear, spoken and shown, with a reason to act now.
8. Compliance: claims, attributes, disclosures, music, AI labels.
9. Producibility: can every visual be shot with the stated budget and people?
10. Pack diversity (ads): do variants differ in concept or hook type, not just wording?

Hard fails whatever the score: an invented stat, review or testimonial; a callout of a viewer's sensitive trait; a cure, guaranteed-result or quick-money claim; a paid creator with no disclosure; unlicensed music in an ad; realistic synthetic people or events with no AI label.
